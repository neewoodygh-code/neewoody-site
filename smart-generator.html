Smart Generator (experimental)

Describe a wardrobe or a kitchen. Every part is placed in 3D first and the cut list is read off the model, so the two always agree and parts that run into each other are flagged.

Kitchen / Laundry

Base units stand on legs. The worktop's doubled 4 cm edge sits flush with the carcass top, so base sides are the worktop height less 40 less the legs. A tall unit stays one box while it fits the board (2300 on legs for a 4 × 8); taller ones are made as two stacked boxes. 4 × 9 and 6 × 9 boards rarely come in laminate, usually chipboard only.

Runs

A run is one line of units along a wall, base or wall cabinets. Units go left to right as you face the wall. On a side wall, "starts at" is measured from the back wall. Drawer heights are shared out to fit the unit. A hob cut-out depth of 0 means no hob; a return depth of 0 means the same depth as the run. A gap leaves space for an appliance. A tall unit's insides are listed bottom to top, separated by commas, with | where the upper box starts: drawer 300 (or drawer 300 x2 side by side), oven 600, micro 450, cubby 300 2x3, shelves 4 (takes the height that is left). Corners: a blind corner is the standard (1000 or 1200 wide; the blind part is the other run's depth plus its door plus an 80 mm filler, closed with a blind panel), and the run that meets it starts with an 80 mm corner filler. The L corner is a special option. Door types: hinged on cup hinges is the usual; lift-ups (on hinges with a gas or mechanical stay, or a hingeless fitting) give one door per shelf space, so the shelves behind them become the door joints; a bi-fold lift folds two panels up; drop-downs (TV units) open down on bottom hinges, with a soft-down stay or without; sliding doors set the carcass back for the Accra tracks. Add "at" to place shelves exactly (shelves 4 at 300 600 900 1200, mm above the bottom of the space); otherwise only the count matters and the installer sets them on site. Shelf heights on other units work the same way. An island is placed by the corner of its cabinets nearest the left and back walls; its sliding-door units are two units back to back; the quartz sits on one panel the size of the top less its 80 mm doubled edge.

Construction (your own settings)

Remembered on this device. The J-profile height is used only when the handles are J-profile. Backs are screwed on; the sink unit has none. Drawer boxes are cut lower than their fronts. Side-mount boxes are the opening less the runner gap, with the base underneath. Undermount boxes follow the Blum rule (Knape & Vogt the same): inside width = the opening less 42, 18 mm sides as standard here (Ghana has no 16 mm laminate board, so use runners made for 18 mm sides, as Temax makes; Blum takes 16 at most), the bottom raised 13 between the sides, the back sitting on the bottom, the box 10 shorter than the runner. Check your runner's own sheet. A J-profile goes on the top edge while that is within reach, otherwise on the bottom edge. Sliding doors run on a flat track below and a bigger track above, inside the depth the carcass is set back by; the side panels stay full depth.
