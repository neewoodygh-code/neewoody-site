# Design review and game plan (2026-11-13)

Owner's aim: **timeless elegance**. Review done after Portfolio, Contact and Hire went live on Astro. Owner approved working through everything **one step at a time**. Nothing below has been started. Tick steps off here as they ship.

How the review was done: read all of `src/`, looked at the homepage and Wardrobes on desktop (1440) and phone (375), plus notes from building the other pages.

## Verdict
The foundation is good, but the site reads as "well-made 2025 website" more than "timeless".

Working well: the bone / espresso / walnut palette with brass used sparingly; real photos and facts; no-JS-safe build; Lighthouse 90s to 100; every product page is one content file on a shared layout.

## Findings

### Visual
1. **Too much motion.** Masked headline rise (`.mask`, `rise`), hero photo `settle` zoom, word-by-word darkening statement (`#mfText`), hover zoom on every photo, Lenis smooth scrolling (`Base.astro`), rotated side caption (`.hero-cap`), parallax classes (`par-slow` / `par-fast`). Together they are this year's award-site kit and will date first. Keep one quiet fade (`.rv`), drop the rest.
2. **Type is thin and scattered.** Cormorant Garamond at weight 300 at very large sizes is fragile. Body text is about 15px (`.92rem` to `.98rem`) in stone grey and reads small on a laptop. `global.css` has **42 distinct font sizes**; aim for six or seven steps defined as tokens.
3. **Tracked capital labels crept back** in ten places (`.label`, `.g-badge`, `.hero-facts dt`, `.hero-cap`, `.proj-cap`, `.contact-meta`, `.foot-links p`, `.g-more`, `.band figcaption`, `.frame-tag`). The owner called these an AI tell before the redesign. Cut to one or two uses.
4. **Photo quality is uneven.** Sage photos are professional; many product photos are phone shots. The Wardrobes hero (`oyarifa` grey laminate) is a flat phone picture filling half the screen. Pick heroes by photo quality; longer term, a proper shoot of three or four jobs would lift the site more than any code.
5. **Footer logo is loud.** `.contact-logo` runs the full page width on every page and fights the quiet serif.
6. **Pages are very long on phones.** Wardrobes at 375px is about 15,600px tall; its gallery alone is 7,368px (12 single-column cards with long captions). Homepage `process` section is 2,431px on phone and has large empty gaps on desktop (step `min-height`).
7. **Two voices.** Homepage copy is spare; product pages still carry the older, wordier SEO text (for example "not borrowed from a showroom"). Should sound like one person. Use the `humanize-copy` skill; keep facts and keywords.

### Structure
1. **Navigation.** `Nav.astro` links are Services / Work (both go back to homepage anchors), Portfolio, Concierge, Contact (`#contact`, the footer, not `/contact`). From a product page you cannot reach another product directly. Concierge serves carpenters, not clients, and sits in the main menu.
2. **Half the site is still the old green design.** All nine `projects/*.html` case studies, `privacy.html`, tools. "Read how we built it" on the new homepage lands on a green page. Most visible inconsistency.
3. **React is loaded for one photo scroller.** `dist/_astro/client.*.js` is about 213 KB for `WorkGallery.jsx` (under 3 KB). Rewrite as plain script in an Astro component, then remove `@astrojs/react`, `react`, `react-dom`.
4. **`src/styles/global.css` is one 465-line file with patches appended** (gallery tones, forms). Move styles into their components; keep only tokens, reset and shared atoms global.
5. **Content files have no checking.** `src/content/*.js` are plain objects; a typo fails at build or silently drops a section. Move to Astro content collections with a schema. Case studies fit this model well. Also the `P` / `ph` helpers are repeated in each file.
6. **Admin-posted projects load in the browser** (`js/cms-posts.js`): invisible to Google, and one is a 5 MB photo from the dispatch R2. Building them into the page at build time touches the dispatch side: **needs the owner's explicit go-ahead in that session** (Standing Instruction 5).
7. **Small Astro wins:** `@astrojs/sitemap` instead of the hand-kept `sitemap.xml`; a per-page share image (Base already takes `ogPhoto`, pages do not pass it); view transitions between pages.
8. Smaller notes from my own work: the viewer's write-up area is cramped (`max-height: 18vh`); Portfolio is three galleries in a row; `data-set` JSON blobs make gallery pages heavy (Portfolio HTML is 64 KB).

## Photo editing (2026-11-14, owner: "Yes, heroes first")
- Tool: `scripts/straighten.py` (needs `numpy` + `opencv-python-headless`; installed to a scratch folder, not the repo). It measures lean from a photo's own vertical lines, removes the roll and 75% of the converging-walls effect, crops back to the same pixel size, and refuses to act when there are too few lines, no clear gain, or the crop would lose more than 14%. `tone()` adds an optional partial white balance and shadow lift. It never alters the furniture.
- Edited in place (same file names and sizes, originals in git history): `bortianor-kitchen-wide`, `bortianor-living-tv-wall` (also less yellow), `bortianor-master-wardrobe`, `achimota-hero-wide`, `golf-hills-installed-6`, `how-hero` (+ `how-gallery-1`, the same photo), `judith-set-installed-2` (shadows lifted only). Before/after sheets: `docs/review/hero-edits-1.jpg`, `-2.jpg`.
- Left alone on purpose: the Sage photos (professional), the three shots looking down at a table (Afromosia, Roman Ridge, East Legon: correcting them would distort the table), Jerksoul and the Dome chest (too few straight lines to measure).
- Honest limits: a light automatic colour clean-up changes little (`docs/review/photo-cleanup-demo.jpg`); the bigger gains are straightening, cropping and how new photos are taken. Removing objects (the cable in the TV wall photo, the protective stickers on the glass wardrobe) needs a retouching tool and the owner's OK to upload photos to it. Not done.
- Next if wanted: run the same tool over gallery photos.

### Gallery photos straightened (2026-11-14, owner: "Yes, galleries")

- **Applied: 91.** Rule used: accept only when the wall-convergence correction is 6 degrees or less at the frame edge. Files edited in place, same names and pixel sizes, re-saved at JPEG quality 76 to 86 so the total stayed at 18.8 MB (18.3 MB before). Originals are in git history (commit before this one).

### Wall repaint trial, Agbogba wardrobe (2026-11-14, owner asked to try it "with Adobe")
- Adobe connector result: not usable for this. `image_select_by_prompt` could not separate the wall from the wardrobe (it selected both, twice) and found nothing for "plaster patches"; the connector has no generative fill or object removal, only flat colour fill. ### Realistic room with Canva's image generator, trial (2026-11-15, owner: "go ahead")
- Method that works: (1) draw the flat room with `scripts/new-room-agbogba.py` so the layout and perspective are fixed, (2) upload that image to Canva (`create-upload-url` + POST) and call Canva `generate-image` with it as the reference and a prompt to keep the wardrobe and make only the room photorealistic, (3) lay the real cutout back over the result with `scripts/composite-room.py`, so the piece shown is the actual photograph. Cutout kept at `docs/review/agbogba-wardrobe-cutout.png`.
- Result: far more realistic than the drawn room (daylight across the wall, real floor with reflections) and Canva kept the wardrobe in place, so the real cutout covers its version. Canva did redraw the wardrobe itself (handles, top doors), which is why the cutout step matters.
- Blocker: through this connection Canva returns only a 149 x 200 preview of the generated image (media id `MAHa-r8AtL0`, real size 1088 x 1456). `import-design-from-url` rejected a plain image, so there was no design to export. The owner needs to download the full-size image from their Canva account; then run `scripts/composite-room.py <download> docs/review/agbogba-wardrobe-cutout.png <out.jpg>`.
- Exact Canva request used (`generate-image`, `aspectRatio: PORTRAIT_3_4`, `imageReferences: [{type: MEDIA, id: <uploaded flat-room image>}]`), prompt: "Turn this reference image into a realistic interior photograph. Keep the built-in wooden wardrobe exactly as it is: same position, size, shape, open doors, shelves, handles and wood grain, same camera angle and framing. Only make the room around it photorealistic: sage green painted walls with soft natural daylight falling across them and gentle shadows in the corners, a real light oak plank floor with subtle reflections and grain, white skirting boards, a plain white ceiling, and a soft realistic shadow where the wardrobe meets the floor and the left wall. No people, no extra furniture, no windows, no text." Change only the wall, floor and piece wording for another photo.
- What is and is not repeatable: the three scripts give the same output every time (they need `numpy`, `opencv-python-headless` and `Pillow`; install them outside the repo). NOT scripted: the cutout (Adobe `image_remove_background`, done through the connector), the Canva generation (a different room each run, even with the same prompt), the owner's manual download of the full-size image, and the room layout points in `new-room-agbogba.py`, which are hand-marked for this one photo and must be re-marked for any other.
- Finished 2026-11-15: the owner downloaded the full-size image into `_inbox` and the composite is `docs/review/agbogba-canva-final.jpg` (1500 x 2000). The real cutout covers Canva's wardrobe cleanly. Known limits: the wardrobe keeps its original lighting, so the daylight on the left wall does not fall on the open door, and Canva's room is 1088 px wide stretched to 1500, so the walls and floor are a little softer than the wardrobe. Review only, not on the site; label it a visualisation if ever published.
- Comparison sheet: `docs/review/agbogba-canva-test.jpg`. Nothing from this trial is on the site. Label any published version as a visualisation.
